# A Bean Batch's Remaining Weight is held per Location and worked out from the Shots pulled there

Each Location could already hold a remaining weight for a batch (ADR-0008), but someone had to type it in and no Shot ever lowered it, so nobody did. #702 makes the number keep itself: a batch's **Remaining Weight** at a Location is the weight its count there starts from, less the dose of each Shot linked to the batch that was pulled at that Location since, plus what Batch Moves have brought there since, less what they have taken away. The count starts from the weight last entered there, or, where the batch had none when a move brought some, from the amount that arrived. It is worked out when it is read (`usedSince`, `movedSince` and `remainingAfter` in `server/src/library/remaining-weight.ts`) and never kept as a running total.

- **One number is stored for a Location, and it is where its count starts.** `batch_locations` keeps what it kept: the weight last entered at the Location, when, and the version of the batch's history that entered it; a Batch Move writes it only where its amount is what the count starts from (Moving a batch, below). Nothing is written as a Shot is stored. So a Shot whose dose is corrected later, that is linked to another batch, that is deleted, or that reaches the server late from a tablet that was offline, changes the weight with no further code: the next read counts the Shots as they then are. A running total would need each of those to find and correct it, on any instance, under the Location's lock.
- **Per Location.** A batch can be at several Locations (ADR-0008), and each has its own weight and its own count. A Shot counts at the Location it was pulled at, which is the Location recorded on the Shot, and nowhere else. A Shot pulled where the batch has no weight subtracts nothing anywhere: at a Location the batch is not at or is untracked at, or on a Machine with no Location.
- **A Shot's dose** is its measured dose where it recorded one, otherwise the dose its Workflow was set to, otherwise nothing. Every Shot linked to the batch counts, Unfinished Shots and Trial Shots included: the coffee was ground.
- **Since the entry.** A Shot counts when it was pulled after the weight was entered, each by its own time: a Shot's by its tablet's clock, an entry on a tablet by that tablet's, and an entry in the management interface or through an assistant by PostgreSQL's (ADR-0016). Entering a weight starts the count again.
- **Untracked is not zero.** A batch with no weight entered at a Location has no Remaining Weight there: it reads null, says "Not tracked", and loses nothing to Shots. Clearing the weight, which is entering none, returns it to that. Batches that had no weight when this shipped stay untracked, and a weight already entered now falls with the Shots pulled since it was entered.
- **Never below zero, and zero through Shots finishes nothing.** Doses are estimates and a weight may be entered too low, so the worked-out weight stops at zero with the batch still at its Location, on its tablets and not Archived.
- **Entering zero finishes.** A weight of zero entered for a Location says the bucket is empty, so it finishes the batch there, as **Finish here** does: in the management interface, over REST, through an assistant, and from a tablet whose barista sets `weightRemaining` to zero. It is one entry of the batch's history, a finish made by a weight of zero (`{ remainingWeight: 0, atLocation: false }`), fields the previous release already reads. A zero entered where the batch is not, as on a tablet's record of a batch finished there, is kept as a weight and finishes nothing again. A zero that arrives in the same change that adds the batch at the Location, as a record created or un-archived on a tablet holding zero, is kept as that tablet has it and finishes nothing. A create or an add that gives a remaining weight of zero through the server is refused as a bad request.
- **A new batch is created at exactly one Location**, so that its roasted weight is counted once, and with a roasted `weight` and no remaining weight its count starts at that weight there, as Decaid's own create does on a tablet. It reaches another Location by being added there.
- **Reads.** `remainingWeight` keeps its name and is now the worked-out weight. Beside it each Location reads with the weight its count starts from, when and by whom (`entered`, with `arrivedFrom`, the Location it arrived from, for a count that started with a move), the number of Shots since and the coffee they used (`used`), and the amounts moved to it and away from it since, in all and by the other Location (`moved`), and the batch with its total over the Locations that track it (`totalRemainingWeight`). A list of batches reads the Shots of all of them in one query and their moves in another, not one per batch.

## Moving a batch between Locations

A **Batch Move** (#704) takes a batch from one Location it is at to another, all of it or part of its weight. It is a request of its own, `POST /api/bean-batches/:id/moves` and the `move_bean_batch` assistant tool, naming the Location left, the Location joined, and all or an amount in grams (`moveBatchBetween` in `server/src/library/location-state.ts`).

- **A move takes from one Location exactly what it gives the other.** An amount lowers the weight at the Location left and raises the weight at the Location joined by the same number, so the sum of a batch's weights over its Locations is the same after a move as before it. No other weight is given with a move. The amount is above zero and below what the Location left has, as worked out when the move is made: one equal to it or above is refused, saying to move all or to enter a corrected weight first.
- **All** takes what the Location left has and finishes the batch there, in the same transaction, as a finish does to its tablets, the Bean's offering, Recipes and Workflows. Its count there ends at zero, so the Location it was finished at reads a weight of zero, and nothing brings back what it had if the batch is added there again.
- **At the Location joined,** a batch not there is added. Where it is there with a weight, the weight rises by the amount. Where it has no weight there, or was not there, the amount arriving is where its count starts, at the time of the move.
- **A move of an amount is kept apart from a weight entered.** It starts neither Location's count again, where both have one: the move is a row of its own, counted with the Shots. So a Shot pulled at the Location left before the move and stored after it, as from a tablet that was offline, still lowers that Location's weight. A weight entered would have started the count again and lost it.
- **The amounts of moves are kept, and no wider ledger.** `batch_moves` holds each move with its two Locations, its amount, its time and its entry in the batch's history, because a Location's weight is worked out from them. It is not a ledger of deliveries and counts: nothing else is a row there, and nothing reads it but the weights.
- **A move counts at a Location by the count it found there, not by a clock.** Each row names the count it was taken from and the one it was added to, by the version that started each (`remaining_weight_version_id`, which every weight entered changes, in this release and the previous one). A move bears on a Location's weight while that is still the Location's count. Comparing a move's time with every entry's would have set PostgreSQL's clock against a tablet's: a weight a barista entered on a tablet whose clock is behind would take a move made before it for one made since. Times are compared in one case only. A move of an amount leaves the weight its count starts from as it was, so a weight a tablet entered before the move, received after it, as from a tablet that was offline, still replaces the count: the moves made since the entry was timed, of that count, are then the new count's, as the Shots pulled since it are. Without them the move would be undone at one Location and kept at the other, and the batch's total would change. A move a count started with is not taken on, as an entry made with it held it already. Through the previous release's server, during a deploy, such an entry does not take the moves on.
- **A count that Shots have overdrawn starts again from what arrives.** A weight stops at zero though its Shots used more than it held. Adding an amount to such a count would give less than arrived, and the batch's total would fall by the difference, so the amount arriving is then where the count starts, as for a batch with no weight there.
- **A batch with no weight at the Location left** has none to split, so the request takes all or "some", with no amount. All finishes it there and adds it at the Location joined. Some adds it at the Location joined and leaves it. Neither moves any weight, and each is kept as a move with no amount. Where either adds the batch at a Location it was finished at, it is untracked there, as nothing arrived: the weight that Location's count was left with is not taken up again. Some to a Location the batch is already at would change nothing, and is refused.
- **One entry of the batch's history.** A move is one version of the batch, at the Location joined, holding what it set there (`atLocation`, `remainingWeight`) and the move itself (`movedFrom`, the id of the Location left, `movedAmount`, `movedAll`), with the account, and the Connected App it came through, or the Machine that made it. Reads give it as `move`, with both Locations by name. There is no undo: a wrong move is corrected by moving back.
- **Who may move.** Staff move a batch when the Location it leaves or the Location it joins is in their scope, so the sender who knows the amount, or the receiver who weighed it, can record it. Adding without moving, finishing, entering a weight and clearing one still need scope at that Location.
- **Locks.** A move changes two Locations' state in one transaction, under both Locations' locks, taken in id order as a batch created at several Locations takes them, before the batch's row. Two moves of one batch at once are made one after the other, on any instance, the second over what the first left.
- **Adding without moving stays.** A tablet whose record was un-archived, a Recipe set to use a batch from another Location or copied with one, and the placement route and `set_location_offering` add the batch and move no weight, as before. The management interface's batch page no longer offers that for a batch that is at a Location: it reaches another by a move.

## Beside the previous release (ADR-0025)

No column or wire message changes, and #704 adds one table, `batch_moves`, which the previous release neither reads nor writes. No data is migrated: a batch at several Locations stays at each, with the weight entered at each. The previous release goes on entering weights and adding and finishing batches at Locations, each stored as before, and this release works the weight out from them. A weight it enters is a new version, so it starts that Location's count again and no move made before it bears on the number. Its pages show a Location's weight as the one its count starts from, without the moves made since, and read a move in a batch's history as the fields of a version at the Location joined. Its batch page still adds a batch at a second Location, moving no weight. A page of this release that reaches the previous release's server has no route to move through, and says the server is being updated. Its pages read the routes they read, whose fields keep their names: they show `remainingWeight`, which this release's server now answers worked out and the previous release's still answers as entered, so the two may show different numbers for a batch for as long as a deploy lasts. The previous release's New Batch dialog can ask for a batch at no Location or at several. This release's REST API still takes that, through either server, each Location starting at the weight given for it or untracked, never at the roasted weight, which would be counted at each; its assistant tool and its own page take one. The release after it refuses none or several over REST too, once no page of the previous release is left to send them.

A zero entered through the previous release's server, from a tablet or from either release's page, is stored as a plain weight as it always was, and finishes nothing, then or after the deploy: the batch stays at the Location reading 0 g until someone finishes it. And the previous release's page fills the weight field it sets from with the `remainingWeight` it reads, now worked out, so pressing **Set** on a batch that Shots have taken to zero, through this release's server, enters zero and finishes the batch there without the warning this release's dialog gives.

## What a tablet holds

A tablet's record of a batch holds the weight its Location's count starts from, as before: the weight last entered there, or the amount a move brought where the batch had none, written to the tablets at the Location joined as a weight entered there is. It does not fall on the tablet as Shots are pulled, or change as a later move adds to it or takes from it: writing the worked-out weight to tablets, and telling such a write from a weight a barista entered, is #705. A move of all archives the batch on the tablets at the Location left, as a finish does.

## Considered options

- **A running total, lowered as each Shot is stored.** Every correction to a Shot, and every late one, would have to find and repair it, and two instances storing Shots at once would have to take the Location's lock to do so.
- **A ledger of Stock Movements**, which the glossary once planned: deliveries, transfers, counts and each Shot's coffee as rows, summed since the last count. The Shots are already rows, and nothing asks for the rest as history, so it is withdrawn. The amounts of moves between Locations are kept, as a Location's weight is worked out from them, and nothing more.
- **A batch at one Location at a time**, which #713 built: adding a batch anywhere finished it everywhere else and carried one weight with it. The owner keeps a sample at the lab and sends the bucket on, so a batch is at several Locations, and is moved between them in part.
- **A move that enters a new weight at each Location.** Both counts would start again at the move, and a Shot pulled before it and stored after it would count nowhere.
- **One weight for the batch, wherever it is.** The owner keeps a sample of a roast at the lab and sends the bucket to a cafe: two places, two weights.
- **Finishing a batch when Shots take it to zero.** A batch a barista is still pulling from would vanish from the tablets because doses are estimates.

## Consequences

- The number is only as good as the doses and the last weighing. Coffee used outside a Shot, as when purging a grinder, is corrected by weighing the bucket and entering the weight.
- A Shot timed by a tablet whose clock is wrong may fall on the wrong side of an entry, and one whose time pulled the server could not work out (`pulled_at`, null where the server could not work it out from the record's times), counts nowhere.
- Reads do more work than before: one grouped query over the Shots linked to the batches being read, by the index on a Shot's batch, and one over their moves.
- A move's amount is only as good as the weight it is taken from. A Shot that reaches the server after a move of all changes nothing, as the Location it was pulled at has no weight by then: the next weight entered where the batch went corrects it.
- A Location deleted takes its state of each batch with it, but not the moves that named it: the amounts they gave another Location still count there, and read as moved from a Location since deleted.
